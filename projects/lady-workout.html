Strength + Curves

A beginner-friendly four-workout sequence built around dumbbells, a bench and a dual-cable machine. Lower body gets the highest growth emphasis, while the upper body focuses on a strong, defined back, rounded shoulders, balanced chest strength and overall fitness.

40–45 min

Day 1 — Glute Volume + Hamstrings

This is the main glute-building session. The priority is progressively getting stronger through hip extension and hinging while also developing the hamstrings and the upper/side glutes.

Goals for this day

  • Build glute size and projection
  • Strengthen hamstrings and the back of the legs
  • Develop the glute-to-thigh transition
  • Improve lower-body strength with simple repeatable movements
  • Finish with targeted cable work rather than replacing the big lifts
01

Dumbbell Hip Thrust

3 × 8–12
Works: Glute max primarily; hamstrings and core assist.

Why it is here: One of the best movements in the plan for adding glute volume and developing strong hip extension.

How to do it
  1. Sit on the floor with your upper back against the edge of a bench.
  2. Place a dumbbell securely across the crease of your hips; use a pad or folded towel if needed.
  3. Plant your feet about hip-width apart with knees bent.
  4. Drive through the whole foot and lift the hips until your torso and thighs form a straight line.
  5. Squeeze the glutes at the top without arching the lower back.
  6. Lower under control and repeat.
Main cue: Ribs down, chin slightly tucked, push through the floor, squeeze the glutes—not the lower back.

02

Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

3 × 8–12
Works: Glutes, hamstrings, spinal erectors and grip.

Why it is here: Builds the back of the legs and glutes while teaching a strong hip-hinge pattern.

How to do it
  1. Stand tall holding a dumbbell in each hand in front of the thighs.
  2. Keep a soft bend in the knees.
  3. Push the hips backward while the dumbbells slide close to the legs.
  4. Keep the spine long and shoulders controlled.
  5. Lower until you feel a strong hamstring stretch without losing position.
  6. Drive the hips forward and stand tall.
Main cue: Hips back, dumbbells close, feel the hamstrings, then squeeze the glutes to stand.

03

Dumbbell Reverse Lunge

3 × 8–10 each leg
Works: Glutes, quads, hamstrings and single-leg stability.

Why it is here: Adds lower-body shape and strength while allowing a slightly more glute-friendly pattern than a forward lunge.

How to do it
  1. Stand tall with a dumbbell in each hand.
  2. Step one foot backward and land softly on the ball of that foot.
  3. Lower both knees under control while keeping most of the pressure through the front foot.
  4. Keep the front knee tracking in line with the toes.
  5. Push through the front foot to return to standing.
  6. Complete the prescribed reps, then switch legs.
Main cue: A slightly longer step and small forward torso lean will usually bring more glute into the movement.

04

Goblet Squat

3 × 10–12
Works: Quads, glutes, adductors and core.

Why it is here: Builds strong legs and glutes with a beginner-friendly squat pattern that is easy to control.

How to do it
  1. Hold one dumbbell vertically against the chest.
  2. Stand around shoulder-width with toes turned out slightly if comfortable.
  3. Brace gently and sit down between the hips.
  4. Keep the whole foot planted.
  5. Descend as far as you can with good control.
  6. Push the floor away and stand tall.
Main cue: Keep the dumbbell close to the chest and let the knees track naturally over the toes.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
05

Cable Hip Abduction

2 × 15–20 each side
Works: Glute medius and upper/side glute.

Why it is here: Adds direct work to the side of the glutes, helping build hip shape and pelvic stability.

How to do it
  1. Attach an ankle strap to the low pulley.
  2. Stand side-on to the machine and hold the frame lightly for balance.
  3. Keep the working leg mostly straight.
  4. Move the leg outward a comfortable distance without leaning the torso.
  5. Pause briefly, then return slowly.
  6. Keep the pelvis facing forward.
Main cue: Use a lighter weight than you think. The goal is side-glute tension, not swinging the leg.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
06

Cable Glute Kickback

2 × 12–15 each side
Works: Glute max, especially hip extension.

Why it is here: A simple isolation exercise to finish the glutes after the heavier compound movements.

How to do it
  1. Attach an ankle strap to the low pulley.
  2. Face the machine and hold it for support.
  3. Keep the standing leg softly bent.
  4. Move the working leg backward from the hip without twisting the pelvis.
  5. Squeeze the glute briefly at the back.
  6. Return slowly without letting the stack slam.
Main cue: Keep the motion coming from the hip. Do not arch the lower back to create extra range.

Day 2 — Back + Shoulders + Upper Chest

This is the main upper-body shaping session. The goal is a defined back, rounded shoulders, stronger posture, and enough chest work to build a strong, balanced upper body without excessive volume.

Goals for this day

  • Build visible upper-back and lat definition
  • Develop rounded, athletic shoulders
  • Strengthen the upper chest and pressing muscles
  • Improve posture through balanced pulling work
  • Add light biceps work for arm shape
01

Cable Lat Pulldown

3 × 8–12
Works: Lats, upper back and biceps.

Why it is here: Builds the sides of the back and helps create a more defined upper-body silhouette.

How to do it
  1. Set both pulleys high and attach suitable handles or a bar.
  2. Sit or kneel far enough back that the cables stay under tension.
  3. Start with arms overhead and shoulders controlled.
  4. Pull the elbows down toward the sides of the ribs.
  5. Pause briefly when the hands reach around upper-chest level.
  6. Return slowly until the lats are lengthened.
Main cue: Think elbows down, not hands down. Avoid turning it into a big backward lean.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
02

Seated Dual-Cable Row

3 × 8–12
Works: Mid-back, lats, rear shoulders and biceps.

Why it is here: Adds thickness and definition through the back while reinforcing strong posture.

How to do it
  1. Set both pulleys around lower-rib height and attach individual handles.
  2. Sit upright on a bench or stable seat.
  3. Start with the arms extended and chest tall.
  4. Pull the elbows backward toward the ribs.
  5. Squeeze the shoulder blades gently together.
  6. Return slowly without rounding forward excessively.
Main cue: Keep the torso mostly still. The elbows move; the body should not rock back and forth.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
03

Incline Dumbbell Press

3 × 8–12
Works: Upper chest, front shoulders and triceps.

Why it is here: Builds upper-chest strength and a balanced front torso while also contributing to shoulder and arm strength.

How to do it
  1. Set the bench to a low or moderate incline.
  2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand and lie back with feet planted.
  3. Start with the dumbbells around upper-chest level.
  4. Press upward and slightly inward.
  5. Stop short of smashing the dumbbells together.
  6. Lower under control until the elbows are comfortably below shoulder level.
Main cue: Keep the shoulder blades supported by the bench and avoid an exaggerated lower-back arch.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
04

Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press

2 × 8–12
Works: Front and side delts, triceps and upper-chest assistance.

Why it is here: Adds general shoulder strength while lateral and rear-delt work supplies most of the shaping volume.

How to do it
  1. Sit against an upright or slightly reclined bench.
  2. Start with dumbbells around shoulder height.
  3. Keep the ribs controlled and feet planted.
  4. Press the dumbbells overhead.
  5. Finish with the arms extended without forcing the shoulders upward.
  6. Lower under control.
Main cue: Do not turn the movement into a backbend. Use the bench for support.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
05

Single-Arm Cable Lateral Raise

3 × 12–20 each side
Works: Side delts.

Why it is here: One of the most useful exercises for developing a rounded shoulder line without needing heavy weights.

How to do it
  1. Set one pulley low and stand side-on to it.
  2. Hold the handle with the outside arm.
  3. Keep a small bend in the elbow.
  4. Raise the arm out to the side until around shoulder height.
  5. Pause briefly.
  6. Lower slowly while keeping tension on the cable.
Main cue: Lead with the elbow and keep the neck relaxed. Do not shrug the weight upward.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
06

Dual-Cable Rear-Delt Fly

2 × 12–20
Works: Rear delts, upper back and external rotators.

Why it is here: Helps create definition at the back of the shoulders and balances all the pressing work.

How to do it
  1. Set the pulleys around shoulder height.
  2. Grab the opposite-side handles so the cables cross in front of you.
  3. Stand tall with soft elbows.
  4. Open the arms outward in a wide arc.
  5. Stop when the upper arms are roughly in line with the body.
  6. Return slowly while keeping tension.
Main cue: Keep the movement in the shoulders rather than squeezing the shoulder blades as hard as possible.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
07

Dumbbell Curl

2 × 10–15
Works: Biceps and forearms.

Why it is here: A small amount of direct biceps work adds arm definition without making arm size a major focus.

How to do it
  1. Stand tall with a dumbbell in each hand.
  2. Keep the upper arms close to the body.
  3. Curl the dumbbells upward without swinging.
  4. Squeeze briefly at the top.
  5. Lower slowly until the elbows are straight.
  6. Repeat with the torso still.
Main cue: If the shoulders move forward or the body starts rocking, the weight is too heavy.

Day 3 — Glutes + Legs + Curves

This session develops the full lower-body shape: glutes, thighs and hamstrings. The emphasis is on controlled squats, split-leg work and hinging, followed by targeted glute work.

Goals for this day

  • Build glutes from multiple movement patterns
  • Develop strong, shaped thighs
  • Strengthen each leg individually
  • Build hamstring and glute definition
  • Improve balance and lower-body control
01

Goblet Squat

3 × 8–12
Works: Quads, glutes, adductors and core.

Why it is here: Provides the main squat pattern for strong legs and glute development without requiring a barbell.

How to do it
  1. Hold one dumbbell close to the chest.
  2. Stand at a comfortable width with toes slightly turned out if needed.
  3. Sit down between the hips while keeping the whole foot planted.
  4. Keep the knees tracking with the toes.
  5. Reach a controlled depth.
  6. Push through the floor to stand.
Main cue: Stay balanced over the whole foot rather than tipping onto the toes.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
02

Dumbbell Bulgarian Split Squat

3 × 8–10 each leg
Works: Glutes, quads and hamstrings.

Why it is here: A powerful single-leg exercise for building glute and thigh shape with relatively little equipment.

How to do it
  1. Stand a comfortable step in front of a bench.
  2. Place the top of the rear foot lightly on the bench.
  3. Hold dumbbells by the sides.
  4. Lower the back knee toward the floor while keeping the front foot planted.
  5. Use a slight forward torso lean if you want more glute emphasis.
  6. Push through the front foot to return.
Main cue: Most of the work should be in the front leg. Adjust the distance from the bench until the movement feels stable.

03

Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

3 × 8–12
Works: Hamstrings, glutes and spinal erectors.

Why it is here: Repeating the hinge pattern twice per four-workout cycle gives the hamstrings and glutes enough practice and growth stimulus.

How to do it
  1. Hold the dumbbells in front of the thighs.
  2. Keep the knees slightly bent.
  3. Push the hips backward.
  4. Keep the dumbbells close to the legs.
  5. Stop when you feel a strong hamstring stretch while maintaining position.
  6. Drive the hips forward and stand tall.
Main cue: Do not squat the dumbbells down. The hips should travel backward.

04

Dumbbell Glute Bridge

3 × 10–15
Works: Glute max with hamstring assistance.

Why it is here: Adds another direct glute-loading movement in a stable position after the harder lower-body exercises.

How to do it
  1. Lie on the floor with knees bent and feet planted.
  2. Place a dumbbell securely across the hips.
  3. Brace lightly and push through the feet.
  4. Lift the hips until the glutes are fully contracted.
  5. Pause briefly.
  6. Lower under control.
Main cue: Finish with the glutes. Do not chase extra height by arching the lower back.

05

Cable Glute Kickback

2 × 12–15 each leg
Works: Glute max.

Why it is here: Adds direct glute work after the compound exercises without creating much overall fatigue.

How to do it
  1. Attach an ankle strap to the low pulley.
  2. Face the machine and hold it lightly.
  3. Keep the pelvis square.
  4. Move the working leg backward from the hip.
  5. Squeeze briefly at the back.
  6. Return slowly.
Main cue: Use a controlled range and keep the lower back quiet.

06

Cable Hip Abduction

2 × 15–20 each leg
Works: Side glutes, especially glute medius.

Why it is here: Finishes the session with direct upper/side-glute work for hip shape and stability.

How to do it
  1. Attach an ankle strap to the low pulley.
  2. Stand side-on and hold the machine lightly.
  3. Move the outside leg away from the body.
  4. Keep the toes mostly forward and pelvis stable.
  5. Pause briefly.
  6. Return slowly.
Main cue: A small clean range is better than a large swinging range.

Day 4 — Upper-Body Shape + Glutes

This is the lighter shaping day. It adds another glute stimulus while giving the back, shoulders, upper chest and arms a second exposure without turning the session into a heavy full-body workout.

Goals for this day

  • Give the glutes a third growth stimulus
  • Add a second dose of back and shoulder work
  • Build upper-chest strength and shape
  • Add light biceps and triceps definition
  • Finish with simple core work
01

Dumbbell Hip Thrust

3 × 10–15
Works: Glute max primarily.

Why it is here: A lighter, higher-rep glute exposure that reinforces the main growth priority without another hard leg session.

How to do it
  1. Place the upper back against a bench.
  2. Position a dumbbell securely across the hips.
  3. Plant the feet and brace lightly.
  4. Drive the hips upward through the whole foot.
  5. Squeeze the glutes at the top.
  6. Lower under control.
Main cue: Use a smooth rhythm and stop the set when the glutes can no longer drive the movement cleanly.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
02

One-Arm Cable Row

3 × 10–12 each side
Works: Lats, mid-back, rear shoulder and biceps.

Why it is here: Provides another back stimulus while letting each side work independently.

How to do it
  1. Set one pulley around lower-rib height.
  2. Stand or sit in a stable position facing the machine.
  3. Start with the arm extended.
  4. Pull the elbow back toward the hip or lower ribs.
  5. Pause briefly.
  6. Return under control without rotating the torso.
Main cue: Keep the chest and hips facing forward while the shoulder and elbow do the work.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
03

Low-to-High Cable Fly

3 × 10–15
Works: Upper chest with front-delt assistance.

Why it is here: Adds chest work using a different line of pull from pressing and is easy to perform with a dual-cable machine.

How to do it
  1. Set both pulleys near the bottom.
  2. Stand slightly forward with one handle in each hand.
  3. Start with the hands low and slightly out to the sides.
  4. Keep a soft bend in the elbows.
  5. Bring the arms upward and inward toward upper-chest height.
  6. Squeeze briefly, then return slowly.
Main cue: Think about bringing the upper arms together. Do not turn it into a straight-arm front raise.

04

Cable Lateral Raise

3 × 12–20 each side
Works: Side delts.

Why it is here: Provides the second shoulder-shaping exposure in the four-day cycle.

How to do it
  1. Set the pulley low.
  2. Stand side-on and hold the handle in the outside hand.
  3. Keep a small bend in the elbow.
  4. Raise the arm outward to around shoulder height.
  5. Pause briefly.
  6. Lower slowly.
Main cue: Keep the shoulder away from the ear and use a weight you can control.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
05

Straight-Arm Cable Pulldown

2 × 10–15
Works: Lats, teres major and core stabilization.

Why it is here: Adds lat definition without requiring another heavy row or pulldown.

How to do it
  1. Set the cable high and attach a rope or straight bar.
  2. Step back and hinge forward slightly.
  3. Start with the arms long in front of you.
  4. Keep the elbows only softly bent.
  5. Pull the handle down in an arc toward the thighs or hips.
  6. Slowly return until the lats are stretched.
Main cue: Keep the arms mostly straight and imagine pushing the handle down using the sides of the back.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
06

Dumbbell Hammer Curl

2 × 10–15
Works: Biceps, brachialis and forearms.

Why it is here: Adds simple arm definition and strength with a joint-friendly neutral grip.

How to do it
  1. Stand tall holding dumbbells with palms facing each other.
  2. Keep the upper arms close to the torso.
  3. Curl the dumbbells upward while keeping the palms neutral.
  4. Pause briefly.
  5. Lower slowly.
  6. Avoid swinging.
Main cue: Keep the wrists straight and the elbows close to the body.
Search this exercise on YouTube ↗
07

Cable Triceps Pressdown

2 × 10–15
Works: Triceps.

Why it is here: Provides enough direct triceps work for arm strength and definition without making arms a major growth priority.

How to do it
  1. Set the pulley high and attach a rope or bar.
  2. Stand tall with elbows close to the ribs.
  3. Start with elbows bent around 90 degrees.
  4. Press the handle down until the arms are straight.
  5. Squeeze briefly.
  6. Return under control without letting the elbows drift forward.
Main cue: Only the forearms should move significantly; keep the upper arms quiet.

08

Cable Crunch

2 × 10–15
Works: Abdominals and deep trunk stabilizers.

Why it is here: Adds simple direct abdominal strength without taking much time.

How to do it
  1. Set the cable high and use a rope attachment.
  2. Kneel facing the machine and hold the rope beside the head.
  3. Keep the hips relatively still.
  4. Curl the ribs downward toward the pelvis.
  5. Pause briefly.
  6. Return slowly without letting the weight pull you upright.
Main cue: Think ribcage toward pelvis rather than elbows toward the floor.

How to progress

Use a weight that makes the prescribed reps challenging while still allowing clean form. Most working sets should finish with roughly 1–3 good reps left.

  1. If the range is 8–12 reps, keep the same weight while reps gradually improve.
  2. Example: 10 / 9 / 8 → 11 / 10 / 9 → 12 / 11 / 10 → 12 / 12 / 12.
  3. Once all sets reach the top of the range with clean technique, increase the weight slightly.
  4. For cable isolation work such as lateral raises, kickbacks and abduction, prioritize control and muscle tension before increasing weight.
  5. Always continue with the next numbered day rather than restarting the sequence because a new week begins.
Designed around general fitness and physique goals. Exercise discomfort should feel like muscular effort, not sharp joint pain.
