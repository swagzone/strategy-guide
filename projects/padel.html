Padel Strength Plan

Three gym days combining hip rehabilitation, bending strength, athletic strength and enough chest, back, shoulder and arm volume to get stronger and look better.

Day 1 felt too light—here is the new target

On the big lifts, finish most working sets with only 1–2 clean reps left. The final two reps should feel challenging while your technique stays controlled. Add weight next session when you comfortably hit the top of the rep range.

How hard to train

  • Main lifts: 1–2 reps in reserve. Rest 2–3 minutes.
  • Upper-body isolation: 0–2 reps in reserve. Rest 60–90 seconds.
  • Hip/core rehab: controlled, never through sharp pain or compensation.
  • When all sets reach the top of the rep range, add the smallest weight increase.
  • If padel performance or next-day hip symptoms worsen, remove one set—not the whole exercise.

Pain and loading rules

Keep familiar hip discomfort at 0–2/10. Reduce range at 3/10. Stop for sharp groin pain, catching, limping, a foot shock, or left lower-back “sticking.” You should be close to your normal baseline the next morning.

Move to Level 2 after 4–6 weeks when:

  • Walking no longer causes the painful push-off transition.
  • No lower-back catching during the strength sessions.
  • Two normal padel sessions do not create a multi-day flare.
  • Split squats and single-leg hinges feel controlled.
  • You are back to baseline the morning after training.
  • A small lying march is comfortable without compensation.
