Simple session formula

4-minute warm-up: easy bike/walk, comfortable shoulder circles, a few hip hinges and unloaded squats (or leg-press practice if ankle is sensitive). Add 1–2 lighter ramp-up sets before the first big lift. Then do the listed exercises. Most working sets stop with 1–3 reps in reserve. Pair A / Pair B exercises if stations are available. No extra rehab circuit is required.

Shoulder progression — train without the jolt

Your normal raises are fine; the thumb-down/internal-rotation raise causes a jolt and position-specific weakness on both sides. We are strengthening comfortable rotations and elevation instead of repeatedly provoking that response.

Advance based on symptoms, not the calendar: move up only after at least two comfortable exposures without the jolt, new numbness, or worse symptoms the next day. If symptoms recur, reduce load/range or return to the previous phase. Full thumb-down cable raises are not a required goal.

Persistent daytime/nighttime numbness and right-hand changes are not explained by this shoulder exercise alone. Arrange an assessment of shoulder, neck and nerve function. Stop exercise that causes jolts, new numbness or progressive weakness.

How hard to train

  • Main lifts: leave ~2 reps in reserve; rest 90–150 seconds on demanding leg movements.
  • Accessories: 2 sets usually suffice. Rest ~45–75 seconds after a comfortable pair of exercises.
  • Hip/shoulder control: very light and smooth. Never chase the jolt, force a thumb-down raise or perform nerve stretches.
  • When all sets comfortably hit the top of the rep range, increase resistance slightly.
  • Busy-day rule: keep the first four exercises, then do the shoulder-control item. Skip optional calves/arms/core if short on time.

Pain and loading rules

Keep the old hip and lower-back movements within comfortable ranges. If your ankle is still painful, swollen or causing a limp, choose stable leg-press options and omit aggravating calf or single-leg work. Stop training through increasing arm numbness, shooting sensations, true weakness, sharp groin pain or catching. Aim to return to baseline by the following morning.

When to progress the plan

  • Most sessions fit comfortably into 35–45 minutes (up to 50 on lower-body days).
  • Padel performance and next-day recovery remain steady.
  • Hip and ankle motions feel controlled and symptom-free.
  • Shoulder-control work stays free of jolts and new numbness or tingling.
  • Any recurrent sensory symptoms are assessed rather than explained away by posture.
