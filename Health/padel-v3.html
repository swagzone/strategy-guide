Padel Strength Plan

Aim for three gym visits weekly (two when padel is busy). Train the whole body each visit, build muscle and padel capacity, and progress shoulder control gradually. Rotate A → B → C without restarting on Mondays.

How to finish within an hour

5–7-minute warm-up: gentle cycle/walk, unloaded hinges and squats, comfortable shoulder movement. Add 1–2 lighter ramp-up sets before the first challenging lift. Do the main legs/hinge first; then superset Pair A (press + row/pulldown) and compatible Pair B/C items when stations are close. Rest 90–120 seconds on heavy legs and about 60–90 seconds between paired rounds. Finish most working sets with 1–3 reps in reserve. Shoulder drills are included—no extra rehab circuit.

Shoulder progression — train without the jolt

You reported a jolt and position-specific weakness during loaded thumb-down/internal-rotation raises on both sides, despite comfortable normal raises. This plan builds shoulder control in tolerable positions; it does not assume guarding is the sole cause.

Progress based on function and symptoms, not the calendar: advance only after several comfortable sessions with no jolt, new numbness, or worse symptoms the next day. If symptoms recur, reduce load/range or return to the previous phase. Full thumb-down cable raises are not a required goal.

Persistent daytime/nighttime numbness and right-hand changes are not explained by this shoulder exercise alone. Arrange an assessment of shoulder, neck and nerve function. Stop exercise that causes jolts, new numbness or progressive weakness.

How hard to train

  • Main lifts: leave ~2 reps in reserve; rest 90–150 seconds on demanding leg movements.
  • Accessories: 2 working sets for most isolation exercises; some optional arm work. Rest ~60–90 seconds after paired rounds.
  • Hip/shoulder control: very light and smooth. Never chase the jolt, force a thumb-down raise or perform nerve stretches.
  • When every working set reaches the top of the rep range with good control and ~2 reps in reserve, increase weight by the smallest practical amount (often 2–5%).
  • Busy-day rule: complete the first four exercises and one comfortable shoulder-control drill. Skip optional arm work or additional accessories if time or recovery is limited.

Pain and loading rules

Keep hip and lower-back work in comfortable ranges. If the recent ankle sprain is still painful, swollen or causing a limp, substitute stable lower-body exercises and do not force split squats. Avoid exercises that cause the familiar electric shoulder jolt, increasing arm numbness or new weakness. Recurring hand/arm numbness needs an in-person assessment; this workout is not a diagnosis.
